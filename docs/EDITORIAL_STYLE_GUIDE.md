# Enclavetools Editorial Style Guide: Smart Brevity

This style guide outlines the signature editorial format for all blog posts, articles, and public announcements on **Enclavetools**.

Our audience consists of busy developers, security engineers, IT admins, CISOs, and legal ops leads. They value high-signal, zero-fluff content that respects their time.

---

## 📐 The 5-Part Smart Brevity Formula

Every blog post must strictly follow these five structural blocks in order:

### 1. HEADLINE
- Must be **fewer than 6 words**.
- Active, punchy, and clear. No clickbait.

### 2. LEDE
- Exactly **one direct sentence** stating the core news or thesis.
- Must immediately tell the reader what happened or what they need to know.

### 3. WHY IT MATTERS
- A single concise paragraph explaining the relevance or business/technical impact.
- **Bold the core key phrase** to enable scanning.

### 4. GO DEEPER
- 3 to 5 concise bullet points containing essential data, statistics, technical details, or auditing facts.
- Bold key terms, maintainer names, and software metrics.

### 5. THE BOTTOM LINE
- Exactly **one summary sentence** delivering the takeaways or actionable conclusion.

---

## ✂️ Constraints & Rules

1. **Cut Word Count by ~50%**: Eliminate introductory filler, conversational transitions, and generic industry jargon.
2. **Use Active Voice**: Prefer *"Engineers audit network calls"* over *"Network calls are audited by engineers"*.
3. **Bold Key Stats & Names**: Make the text scannable at a glance by bolding important technologies (`llama.cpp`), metrics (`100% offline`), and project names.
4. **Accuracy in Claims**: Always state audit findings factually (e.g., *"transparently labeled"*, *"audited telemetry SDKs"*) rather than making false claims.
